Does occupancy track productivity? — the matching correlation, live

At the Ideal Free Distribution, headcount on each patch is proportional to how much it produces, so the correlation reaches 1. The race below is the whole claim: it could have ended in a tie.

Event ledger

Method. A fixed flock — no births, no deaths (energy and water floored each tick, so the same 44 foragers persist and stay hungry). Vegetation regenerates and depletes, so a crowded patch pays less per forager: the negative feedback the distribution rides on. The only difference between the plates is the crowd discount in the movement rule. Robustness. Ideal-free beats greedy on every seed 0–5 (greedy matching r̄ ≈ 0.47, ideal-free r̄ ≈ 0.90); it reaches near-perfect matching, r ≈ 1.0, on four of the six. Seed 0 is shown, representative of that majority; on the two harder seeds the advantage is smaller (r ≈ 0.65–0.77) but present, and greedy coordinates on none. Foragers undermatch — they slightly under-fill the richest patch, slope ≈ 0.4 — exactly as real animals do in Fretwell–Lucas / Milinski feeder experiments. Nothing in the rule names patches, counts foragers, or targets a distribution; the order is an unplanned by-product of each forager pursuing its own best meal.
