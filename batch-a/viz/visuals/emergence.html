Case C2′ · emergence · the invisible hand, put to selection

The coordinated peak exists. Evolution walks the other way.

Agents evolve a foraging policy from two raw senses — food, and nearby others — with nothing about crowds written in. The genome that produces a fair distribution is right there. Selfish selection never climbs to it.

How to read it. The curve is the matching correlation (occupancy vs productivity — 1 = the Ideal Free Distribution) that a flock reaches if every agent carries a given crowd-gene gcrowd. The peak is on the left: agents that weight nearby others negatively (avoid crowds) spread to match the food and coordinate almost perfectly. Now let selection run (founders born blind, at 0): the red path is the evolving population's own gene and coordination. It marches right — toward crowd-following — because food and crowds sit in the same place, so “avoid crowds” means “avoid food,” which starves the avoider. The individually best move is toward food, which is toward others. The coordinated genome is a real peak that incremental selfish selection cannot reach from the blind start; it must be authored. Innocent rules, a collective outcome no one chose and all are worse for — Schelling and Calhoun, not Smith. Caveat: these runs sat at the population cap, which weakens selection’s strength; the direction is unanimous across regimes and mechanistically forced, but a genuinely food-limited world (smaller patches) would sharpen the claim.
